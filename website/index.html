AN EXPERIMENT IN INTERPRETATION

How does the language score?

VADER SENTIMENT

A dictionary-based model estimates positive, neutral, or negative language in each matching event narrative. These scores are exploratory: damage descriptions can score negative even when the writer is factual. They do not measure severity, risk, or the writer’s feelings.

How are scores calculated?

VADER 3.3.2 uses word sentiment, punctuation, capitalization, and rules such as negation. Its compound score ranges from −1 to +1. Scores ≥ +0.05 are labeled positive, ≤ −0.05 negative, and the rest neutral. Scores are not probabilities. VADER was designed for social media and has not been validated on this NOAA sample. Each record counts once; no matching records means no distribution. Event narratives only; episode context is excluded.

How are distinctive words selected?

Each word counts once per event narrative. We subtract the other group’s share of narratives mentioning that word from this group’s share, then rank the positive differences. Only words appearing in at least two narratives in this group are included; the top 10 are shown. The gap is measured in percentage points (pp). We use the same common-word filtering as the word clouds and exclude episode context. These descriptive differences are not tests of statistical significance. Small groups and longer reports can still skew the results.

What do the labels mean?

Places include geopolitical areas, natural features, and facilities. Other named entities include named events, products, and works. Hover over a highlight or focus it with your keyboard to see its model label. Numeric quantities are left unhighlighted. A person or language label is adjusted to a place when the entire name matches a location field in the same NOAA record, ignoring case and repeated spaces. Highlight tooltips identify these adjustments. This context rule can still be wrong; labels are not verified facts.
